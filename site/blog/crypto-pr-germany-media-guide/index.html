Crypto PR in Germany: the media landscape and how to earn coverage

Crypto PR in Germany: which media matter, from BTC-ECHO to Handelsblatt, what German journalists expect and how crypto brands earn coverage that builds trust.

Key takeaways

  • Crypto PR in Germany works across three media tiers – crypto-native, business and finance, and tech – plus separate outlets in Austria and Switzerland.
  • German journalists expect a local angle, real data, a German-speaking spokesperson and clarity on regulatory status; hype gets ignored.
  • Sponsored articles and advertorials must be labeled as advertising; earned coverage builds more trust but needs a real story.
  • Measure crypto PR by backlinks, branded search and referral traffic, not by the number of clippings.

Crypto PR in Germany means earning coverage in a small but influential set of German-language media – crypto-native outlets, business and finance titles and tech media – with stories that have a local angle, solid data and a credible German-speaking voice. Hype-driven press releases rarely get picked up; concrete news, regulatory clarity and real expertise do. Here is how the German crypto media scene is structured and how to earn coverage that builds trust.

German-language crypto media: three tiers plus Austria and Switzerland

Tier Examples Typical topics What coverage gives you
Crypto-native BTC-ECHO, Cointelegraph Deutsch Market news, projects, regulation, education Reach among active crypto users, credibility in the scene
Business & finance Handelsblatt, finanzen.net Markets, companies, investing, regulation Trust with mainstream investors and decision-makers
Tech & startups t3n Digital business, startups, technology Reach among tech-savvy early adopters
Austria Der Brutkasten, Trending Topics Startups, tech, innovation Visibility in the Austrian ecosystem
Switzerland finews.ch Finance industry Access to the Swiss financial community

Crypto and comparison sites such as Bitcoin2Go and Bitcoin-Kurier sit between media and partners. For Waltio, they were partnership channels for visibility, discovery and backlinks.

Where to start? Usually with crypto-native media, where you build credibility in the scene. Business media become realistic once you have news with broader relevance – an authorization, proprietary market data or a clear consumer angle.

What German journalists expect

  • A local angle: what does your news mean for users in Germany? A global announcement plus "now also available in Germany" is not a story.
  • Data and substance: numbers from your own product, documented surveys or concrete market observations.
  • A German-speaking spokesperson: someone who gives interviews in German and answers follow-up questions quickly.
  • Regulatory clarity: your MiCA status and BaFin-relevant facts, stated plainly. Journalists will ask.
  • No hype: superlatives, price talk and buzzwords make a pitch read like advertising – and editors treat it that way.

Press release vs. story pitching

Press releases still have a job. They create a dated, citable record of facts – a launch, an authorization, a partnership – and distribute it through press portals and newswires. But a release alone rarely earns editorial coverage.

Story pitching does. You offer one outlet a specific angle, exclusive data or an expert interview that fits its readers. It takes more work per outlet and usually delivers better results. Strong campaigns combine both: the release as the factual anchor, pitches for the stories around it.

A launch PR sequence for Germany

  1. Before launch: prepare a German press kit – fact sheet, regulatory status, spokesperson bio, visuals – and a target list by tier. Make sure your German landing page is live, so coverage has somewhere to send readers.
  2. Launch week: publish the release as the factual record, offer one key outlet an early briefing or an exclusive angle, and line up interviews with your German-speaking spokesperson.
  3. The following weeks: pitch follow-up stories with data, expert commentary on regulation or tax questions, and guest articles for crypto-native media.
  4. Ongoing: become the source journalists call when MiCA, BaFin or tax topics come up.

Waltio's launch PR shows what a well-timed campaign can carry: built around the German launch and including a Handelsblatt press-portal placement, it generated 70+ media publications.

Earned vs. sponsored coverage

Not every placement is editorial. Many outlets also sell sponsored articles, advertorials and press-release distribution. These can help with reach, SEO and launch visibility – but they are advertising and must be labeled as such, for example as "Anzeige" or "Werbung". Readers and journalists notice the difference.

Rule of thumb: use sponsored placements for distribution and SEO, earned coverage for credibility. Never present a paid article as independent coverage in your own marketing; under German advertising law and MiCA, that risks being misleading.

Timing hooks that make news

  • Launch: your German market entry, a German product version or a local partnership.
  • Regulation: a MiCA authorization, BaFin announcements or regulatory milestones – with your expert view on what they mean for users.
  • Tax season: crypto tax questions move up the agenda when investors prepare their returns – a natural hook for tax tools, exchanges and portfolio trackers.
  • Market moments: sharp price moves or major industry events, if you can add more than price talk.
  • Your own data: aggregated, anonymized insights about German users of your platform.

For tax products, timing is everything: Waltio's PR and content were timed around tax-season demand. Our article on tax-season marketing shows how to plan for it.

Measuring PR beyond clippings

A list of clippings shows activity, not impact. Track these instead:

  • Backlinks: links from German-language media strengthen your rankings for German keywords.
  • Branded search: searches for your brand name from Germany after coverage appears.
  • Referral traffic: visits and signups from articles, tracked by source.
  • Share of voice: how often you appear alongside competitors in relevant coverage.
  • Message pull-through: whether articles carry your key messages correctly.

PR works best together with content. For Waltio, launch PR ran alongside a German SEO foundation of 4 main guides, 2 tax calculators and 10 blog posts, plus partnerships with crypto and comparison sites for backlinks – and Waltio reached #3 in organic keyword rankings for crypto tax topics within a few months. The details are in our Waltio case study, and our market-entry playbook shows where PR fits in the wider channel mix.

FAQ

Which media matter most for crypto PR in Germany?

It depends on your audience. Crypto-native outlets such as BTC-ECHO and Cointelegraph Deutsch reach active crypto users; business and finance media such as Handelsblatt and finanzen.net reach mainstream investors; t3n covers tech and startups. For Austria and Switzerland, add outlets such as Der Brutkasten, Trending Topics and finews.ch. Most campaigns start with crypto-native media and expand once there is broader news.

Do I need a German-speaking spokesperson?

It helps a lot. German journalists prefer interviews and quotes in German, and a spokesperson who can explain your product, regulatory status and local plans without translation delays is more likely to be quoted. If your team has no German speaker yet, a local representative or advisor can take on the role at the start.

How much does crypto PR in Germany cost?

It depends on scope: how many outlets and markets you target, whether you add sponsored placements to earned coverage, and how much content and spokesperson support you need. Earned coverage costs time and expertise rather than placement fees; sponsored articles and distribution are priced by each outlet. Plan PR as an ongoing workstream, not a one-off release.

How long does it take to get media coverage?

A strong, well-timed story can be picked up within days, especially around launches or regulatory news. Building steady relationships with German journalists takes months. Plan a sequence – launch announcement, follow-up stories with data or expert commentary, and seasonal hooks such as tax season – rather than expecting one release to carry the campaign.
