Crypto tax season in Germany: a marketing calendar for crypto brands

Crypto tax season Germany: when demand for tax tools and guides peaks, which content works, and a quarter-by-quarter marketing calendar for crypto brands.

Crypto tax season in Germany is the stretch in the first half of the year when investors prepare their tax returns – and interest in crypto tax reports, guides and tools rises. For tax tools – and for exchanges and wallets with tax features – it is one of the most predictable demand peaks in the German market. The brands that win it start preparing in Q4 of the previous year, not when the first users ask in March.

Why tax season shapes crypto demand in Germany

At tax time, German crypto investors need more than a portfolio overview. Anyone who sold or swapped crypto during the year needs a clean record of those transactions, often across several exchanges and wallets. That creates demand for three things:

  • Reports: tools that import transactions and produce a report users or their tax advisor can work with.
  • Explanations: German users research before they decide – they read guides, watch explainer videos and check comparison sites.
  • Trust: when money and the tax office are involved, users prefer brands that look established, transparent and local.

Because this demand is seasonal, you can plan for it like a product launch. And because major ad platforms restrict crypto advertising, organic search, PR, creators and partnerships carry most of the load – all channels that need lead time.

German crypto tax basics, in plain words

You don't need to be a tax expert to market in tax season, but your content has to get the basics right:

  • Holding period: for private individuals, gains from selling crypto held for more than one year are tax-free under § 23 EStG. Germans often call this the "Spekulationsfrist".
  • Short-term gains: gains on crypto held for one year or less stay tax-free only if total gains from such private sales are below €1,000 per year – an exemption limit that has applied since 2024. If they reach €1,000, the full amount is taxed at the personal income tax rate.
  • Deadlines: filing deadlines depend on the case, for example whether someone files alone or with a tax advisor. Point readers to the tax office or a tax advisor for current dates.
  • Reporting: under the EU's DAC8 directive, which applies from 2026, crypto-asset service providers report customer transaction data to tax authorities – one more reason investors want their numbers right.

Two marketing rules follow. First, "tax-free after one year" is true but incomplete, so avoid shortcuts like "no tax on crypto". Second, individual tax advice in Germany is reserved for authorized professionals such as tax advisors (Steuerberater), so keep your content educational and bring tax advisors in for expert formats.

This is a simplified overview, not tax advice. Rules change – check current law or ask a tax advisor.

The crypto tax season marketing calendar

Quarter Focus Key activities
Q4 (Oct–Dec) Prepare German keyword research; guides and calculators published; comparison-site and creator partnerships agreed; PR angles and tax experts lined up
Q1 (Jan–Mar) Educate & launch Guides and calculators updated for the new tax year; launch PR; comparison-platform listings live; weekly expert videos
Q2 (Apr–Jun) Peak German support at full capacity; creator content and live Q&As; content almost daily across channels
Q3 (Jul–Sep) Recap & evergreen Rankings and conversions reviewed; evergreen content updated for rule changes; partnerships renewed

Q4: build before anyone searches

Search engines need time to index and rank new pages, and partners need time to plan. Publish your evergreen foundation – guides, calculators, FAQ pages – and agree on listings, creator collaborations and expert partners now.

Q1: educate and launch

Update every guide for the new tax year and launch PR around something useful – a new calculator, a guide, an expert panel. Get comparison-platform listings live before users start comparing.

Q2: peak support and creator content

This is when questions turn into sign-ups. Staff German-language support for peak volume, run creator explainers and live Q&As, and publish short, useful content across channels at a high frequency.

Q3: recap and evergreen updates

Analyze which pages, partners and creators drove sign-ups, update evergreen content for rule changes and renew what worked. Then the cycle starts again.

Content formats that work in tax season

  • Calculators: simple tools such as a crypto tax or holding-period calculator answer one concrete question and attract links.
  • Guides: evergreen guides on how crypto is taxed, how to report it and what counts as a taxable event – updated every year.
  • Expert videos: German tax experts on camera answering real user questions build trust that marketing copy can't.
  • Comparison-platform listings: German users compare before they choose, and good placements on crypto comparison sites put you on the shortlist.
  • Creator content and PR: explainers by German crypto creators and press coverage add reach and third-party credibility. Paid creator posts must be clearly labeled as advertising ("Werbung" or "Anzeige") – more in our guide to crypto influencer marketing in Germany.

Advertising labels are a legal requirement in Germany – this is not legal advice.

Austria and Switzerland: different rules, different messages

Tax content doesn't travel across DACH borders:

  • Austria: since 2022, crypto income has been taxed at a special rate of 27.5%. The German message around the one-year holding period doesn't carry over, so Austrian users need their own guides. The Austrian Federal Ministry of Finance publishes official information.
  • Switzerland: private capital gains are generally tax-free for private investors, but crypto holdings are subject to wealth tax. Swiss content therefore centers on declaring holdings and their year-end values.

Waltio: a tax-season go-to-market in practice

Waltio, France's #1 crypto tax tool with 150,000+ users, set out to establish itself among Germany's leading crypto tax providers – in a market held by local incumbents with a decade of brand, SEO and industry relationships. Over 6–8 months, we built and ran Waltio's German go-to-market, timed around tax-season demand:

  • SEO foundation: 4 main guides, 2 tax calculators and 10 blog posts – evergreen German content built ahead of tax season.
  • Launch PR: 70+ publications, including a Handelsblatt press-portal placement.
  • Partnerships: crypto and comparison sites such as Bitcoin2Go, Bitcoin-Kurier and MissCrypto for visibility, discovery and backlinks.

When tax season peaked, we pushed out content almost daily across channels. The results: #3 in organic keyword rankings for crypto tax topics within a few months, Top 3 rankings on selected comparison platforms, 1M+ impressions and 10,000+ views on Waltio's German YouTube channel. FAQ

When is crypto tax season in Germany?

There is no official start date. Interest in crypto tax topics rises in the first half of the year, when investors prepare their tax returns for the previous year. Filing deadlines depend on the individual case, so check current dates with the tax office or a tax advisor (Steuerberater). For marketing, that means: prepare in Q4.

What content works best in crypto tax season?

Content that helps users get their taxes done: calculators, step-by-step guides, explainer videos with German tax experts and strong comparison-platform listings. Calculators and guides earn rankings and links if they are live before demand peaks. At the peak, a steady stream of short, useful content beats one big campaign.

Can a foreign crypto tax tool compete with German incumbents?

It can, with the right timing and local credibility. Waltio entered a German market held by incumbents with a decade of brand and SEO history. With German guides and calculators ready before tax season, launch PR, comparison-site partnerships and weekly expert videos, it reached #3 in organic keyword rankings for crypto tax topics within a few months.

Are crypto gains tax-free in Germany?

For private individuals, gains from selling crypto held for more than one year are generally tax-free under § 23 EStG. Short-term gains stay tax-free only if total gains from private sales are below the €1,000 annual exemption limit; if they reach it, the full amount is taxed at the personal income tax rate. Special cases exist – this is not tax advice.
