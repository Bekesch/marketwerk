The DACH crypto market: Germany, Austria and Switzerland compared

The DACH crypto market compared: regulators, MiCA status, currency, language and tax basics in Germany, Austria and Switzerland – and where to start.

Key takeaways

  • The DACH crypto market spans Germany, Austria and Switzerland: 100M+ German speakers, but three regulators, two currencies and different tax rules.
  • Germany and Austria are EU members under MiCA, supervised by BaFin and the FMA; Switzerland is outside the EU and regulated by FINMA.
  • Germany is the largest market and the toughest test: conservative, research-heavy users who prefer regulated providers and German-language support.
  • For most international crypto brands, the right sequence is Germany first, then Austria and Switzerland with local adjustments – and from there France, Spain and Italy.

The DACH crypto market – Germany (D), Austria (A) and Switzerland (CH) – gives crypto brands access to 100M+ German speakers, but it is not one uniform market. Germany and Austria are EU members under MiCA, supervised by BaFin and the FMA; Switzerland sits outside the EU, is regulated by FINMA and has its own currency and spelling. For most international crypto brands, the smart sequence is Germany first, then Austria and Switzerland with targeted local adjustments.

Size points the same way: Germany has more than 80 million inhabitants, Austria and Switzerland around 9 million each. That's why Germany works as the anchor market and its neighbors as targeted extensions.

The tax row is simplified for comparison – this is not tax or legal advice.

Germany: the largest market and the toughest test

Germany is Europe's largest economy and one of its biggest crypto markets. It is also where crypto brands meet the most demanding users in the region.

  • Regulation: MiCA has applied in full since 30 December 2024, with BaFin as the competent authority. Germany chose a shorter transitional period for existing providers than the EU maximum, which ran until 1 July 2026 – so crypto-asset service providers now generally need a MiCA authorization, from BaFin or passported from another EU member state. Check BaFin for the details that apply to you.
  • Users: in our experience, German crypto users are privacy-conscious, research-heavy and conservative. They read the fine print, compare fees and security, prefer regulated providers and expect German-language support.
  • Media and creators: an established German-language crypto media scene – BTC-ECHO, Cointelegraph Deutsch, finanzen.net, t3n and Handelsblatt for business coverage – plus education-focused creators such as Blocktrainer.
  • Tax-driven demand: the one-year holding rule and the annual tax return create a predictable season of demand for tax content and tools – see our crypto tax season calendar.

General information, not legal or tax advice.

Austria: EU rules, its own scene

Austria shares Germany's EU framework but has its own market character.

  • Regulation: MiCA applies, with the FMA as supervisor. A MiCA authorization from another EU member state can be passported into Austria.
  • Ecosystem: Austria is the home market of Vienna-based Bitpanda, so international brands compete with a local name Austrian users already know.
  • Media: German outlets reach Austrian readers too, but Austria has its own startup and tech media such as Der Brutkasten and Trending Topics.
  • Tax and language: crypto income has been taxed at a special rate of 27.5% since 2022, so tax content needs its own version. And in Austrian German, January is "Jänner".

Not legal or tax advice – check details with the FMA or an Austrian tax advisor.

Switzerland: outside the EU, home of Crypto Valley

Switzerland is the most distinct of the three markets.

  • Regulation: Switzerland is not in the EU, so MiCA doesn't apply and an EU authorization has no passporting effect there. FINMA supervises under Swiss financial market law, which the country adapted early with its DLT legislation, in force since 2021.
  • Ecosystem: the "Crypto Valley" around Zug is one of Europe's best-known blockchain hubs.
  • Currency and language: prices are in CHF, Swiss Standard German writes "ss" instead of "ß", and French, Italian and Romansh are national languages too.
  • Media: Swiss financial outlets such as finews.ch reach a finance-savvy audience.
  • Tax: private capital gains are generally tax-free for private investors, but crypto holdings are subject to wealth tax.

Not legal or tax advice – Swiss taxation depends on the canton and individual circumstances.

How to sequence your DACH go-to-market

"Start in Germany. Scale across Europe." In practice, that means:

  1. Germany first. The largest audience and the highest expectations. If your positioning, compliance and content hold up here, they have passed Europe's toughest test – see our guide on how to enter the German crypto market.
  2. Austria next. Reuse most German assets under the same EU framework; adapt tax content and vocabulary, and add Austrian media and partners.
  3. Switzerland with its own checklist. A separate regulatory review, CHF pricing, Swiss spelling and Swiss media – plus French content if you target the Romandie.
  4. Then scale. With proof from DACH, our network in France, Spain and Italy lets you launch campaigns there right away.

What carries over – and what doesn't

  • Carries over: your German brand voice, most evergreen content and community channels, plus German-language creators and media whose audiences often reach into Austria and Switzerland.
  • Needs local work: tax content, regulatory statements, currency and pricing, Swiss spelling, and local media and partners in each country.

France's #1 crypto tax tool built its German presence with an SEO foundation, launch PR with 70+ publications, partnerships with crypto and comparison sites and weekly YouTube videos with German tax experts – and reached #3 in organic keyword rankings for crypto tax topics within a few months. FAQ

What does DACH stand for?

DACH stands for Germany (D), Austria (A) and Switzerland (CH), based on the countries' international vehicle registration codes. In marketing, it describes the German-speaking core of Central Europe, with 100M+ German speakers. The three countries share a language but differ in regulation, currency and tax rules, so a DACH strategy needs local adjustments rather than one copy-paste campaign.

Which DACH country should a crypto brand enter first?

For most international brands, Germany. It is the largest market in the region, sets the toughest standards for trust and compliance, and German-language content can later be adapted for Austria and Switzerland. Austria usually follows with small adjustments under the same EU framework, while Switzerland needs a separate regulatory check, CHF pricing and Swiss spelling. The main exception: products built specifically for Swiss users.

Does MiCA apply in Switzerland?

No. Switzerland is not an EU member, so MiCA does not apply there, and a MiCA authorization from an EU country has no passporting effect in Switzerland. Crypto businesses are supervised by FINMA under Swiss financial market law, including the country's DLT legislation. Plan a separate regulatory review before you actively target Swiss users. This is general information, not legal advice.

How is crypto taxed in Germany, Austria and Switzerland?

Differently. In Germany, private gains on crypto held for more than one year are generally tax-free, and short-term gains are tax-free only below a €1,000 annual exemption limit. Austria has taxed crypto income at a special rate of 27.5% since 2022. In Switzerland, private capital gains are generally tax-free, but crypto holdings are subject to wealth tax. Details depend on individual circumstances – this is not tax advice.
