MiCA marketing rules: what crypto brands can (and can't) say in Germany

MiCA marketing rules explained: what crypto marketing in Germany must include, what to avoid, how BaFin and German ad law fit in, plus a checklist.

Key takeaways

  • Under MiCA, crypto marketing communications must be clearly identifiable as marketing, fair, clear and not misleading, and consistent with the white paper where one is required.
  • MiCA's marketing rules sit in Art. 7 for most crypto-assets, Art. 29 for asset-referenced tokens, Art. 53 for e-money tokens and Art. 66 for crypto-asset service providers.
  • Treat paid creator content as your own marketing: in Germany it needs a clear label such as “Werbung” or “Anzeige” and must meet the same MiCA standards.
  • No crypto ad should suggest that BaFin or any other authority has approved the ad or endorses the product.

MiCA marketing rules require crypto marketing communications in the EU – and therefore in Germany – to be clearly identifiable as marketing, fair, clear and not misleading, and consistent with the crypto-asset white paper where one is required. Crypto-asset service providers must meet the same standard for all client information, marketing included. BaFin supervises these rules in Germany, and German advertising law adds its own labeling duties.

What MiCA is and why marketers should care

The Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation – Regulation (EU) 2023/1114, or MiCA – is the EU's harmonized framework for crypto-assets. Its rules for asset-referenced and e-money tokens have applied since 30 June 2024, the rest since 30 December 2024 – directly, in every member state including Germany.

MiCA governs not only who may offer crypto-assets and services, but also how they are promoted – which makes marketing part of compliance and of any German go-to-market plan. Social posts, creator videos, landing pages, newsletters, press releases and event talks can all be marketing communications.

Which MiCA provisions cover marketing

MiCA has no single marketing chapter. The rules that apply depend on what you offer:

  • Art. 7: marketing for offers to the public or admissions to trading of crypto-assets other than asset-referenced tokens (ARTs) and e-money tokens (EMTs).
  • Art. 29: marketing by issuers of ARTs.
  • Art. 53: marketing by issuers of EMTs.
  • Art. 66: information duties of crypto-asset service providers (CASPs), marketing included.

Art. 7 spells out what this means. Marketing under it must refer to the published white paper, give the website, phone number and email address of the party responsible, and carry a clear, prominent statement that no competent authority has reviewed or approved it and that the offeror alone is responsible for its content. Where a white paper is required, no marketing may be disseminated before it is published. Art. 29 and Art. 53 set parallel rules for stablecoin issuers, including a clear statement on holders' right of redemption.

The core principles in practice

  • Clearly identifiable: users must recognize marketing at first glance – visible labels on sponsored posts, advertorials and creator content, not a disclosure hidden at the end of a caption.
  • Fair, clear and not misleading: no promised returns, no "risk-free" yields, no cherry-picked performance periods, no fees buried in the fine print. MiCA also bars CASPs from misleading clients, deliberately or negligently, about the real or perceived advantages of crypto-assets.
  • Consistent with the white paper: every claim about features, rights, rewards or risks must match it. If the white paper says rewards are variable, your ad cannot present them as fixed.
  • Balanced risk information: if you mention rewards, yields or price history, the risks belong right next to them. CASPs must also warn clients of the risks of crypto-asset transactions.
  • No implied regulatory approval: you may state an authorization accurately, but it is not a quality seal. Never suggest that an authority approved your ad, endorses your product or makes an investment safe.

What you can and can't say: examples

Avoid Why it's a problem Say instead
"Passive income with zero risk" Promises returns, hides risk "Rewards vary, and you can lose some or all of your capital."
"BaFin-approved token" Implies regulatory endorsement "[Company] is authorized by BaFin as a crypto-asset service provider" – only if true
"Germany's safest exchange" Unverifiable superlative The specific security measures you actually use
"This token will double after listing" Price prediction Utility and rights as described in the white paper
"#ad" at the end of a hashtag list Not clearly identifiable "Werbung" or "Anzeige", visible at the start

Influencer and affiliate content

The practical question is not who publishes a message, but who is behind it. Treat any content you pay for, brief or incentivize – fees, affiliate commissions, free tokens, referral bonuses – as your own marketing communication, held to the same standards:

  • Write MiCA requirements and off-limits claims into every creator and affiliate brief.
  • Give creators an approved fact sheet: features, fees, risks and authorization status.
  • Review content before it goes live, and archive briefs, approvals and final posts.
  • Keep affiliate networks under control: sub-affiliates must not invent their own claims.

Education-first formats also make compliance easier: Waltio's German creator work relied on explainers and expert videos (Waltio case study). Our guide to crypto influencer marketing in Germany shows how to build a creator program that converts within these rules.

German specifics: BaFin, ad labeling, platforms and transition

BaFin as competent authority

BaFin supervises MiCA in Germany, and the Crypto Markets Supervision Act (Kryptomärkteaufsichtsgesetz, KMAG) supplements the regulation nationally. For authorization status and current guidance, check bafin.de and ESMA's MiCA register on esma.europa.eu.

Ad labeling under German law

Independent of MiCA, German unfair competition law (UWG) and media law require commercial content to be recognizable as such. For sponsored posts and creator content, use clear German labels such as "Werbung" or "Anzeige" where users look first. English shorthand like "#ad" is risky with a German audience.

Ad platforms

Major ad platforms restrict crypto advertising and require licensing or authorization in many EU markets – Google, for instance, requires MiCA authorization for many crypto ads in the EU. Check each platform's current policy before planning paid campaigns.

Transitional periods

MiCA allowed member states to give existing providers a transitional period until 1 July 2026 at the latest, and Germany opted for a shorter one. These periods have now ended, so providers serving clients in Germany must operate under MiCA. To check a specific provider's status, go to BaFin.

Pre-publication checklist

Run every asset – ad, landing page, creator script, press release – through these checks before it goes live:

  1. Labeling: Is it recognizable as marketing at first glance, with "Werbung" or "Anzeige" where needed?
  2. Accuracy: Is every claim true, specific and verifiable today?
  3. White paper: Does it match the white paper on features, rights, rewards and risks?
  4. Mandatory elements: Are the references, contact details and statements required for your type of crypto-asset included?
  5. Risks: Do risks appear next to every mention of returns, rewards or performance?
  6. Authorities: Does anything imply approval or endorsement by BaFin or another authority?
  7. Targeting: Is the audience covered by your authorization and the platform's policy?
  8. Records: Are the brief, approval and final version archived?

This article is general information about MiCA and German advertising rules, not legal advice. Rules and supervisory practice evolve – work with qualified legal counsel and check BaFin's current guidance before you publish.

FAQ

Do MiCA marketing rules apply to influencers?

In practice, yes: once you pay for, brief or otherwise incentivize content, treat it as your own marketing communication. It must be clearly labeled, must not mislead and must match your white paper, with German labeling duties on top. Responsibility does not disappear because a creator posts it. This is general information, not legal advice.

Does BaFin have to approve crypto ads in advance?

Generally not. For most crypto-assets, MiCA does not provide for prior approval of marketing communications – which is why Art. 7 requires a statement that no authority has reviewed or approved them. Authorities can still act against marketing that breaks the rules, so responsibility for every claim stays with you. This is general information, not legal advice.

Does MiCA apply in Austria and Switzerland?

In Austria, yes: MiCA applies directly, and the FMA is the competent authority. Switzerland is not in the EU and not covered by MiCA; FINMA supervises there under Swiss rules. If you market across DACH, review each country separately – including ad labeling, which follows the same spirit in all three.

Can I mention my BaFin authorization in marketing?

Yes, if the statement is accurate and complete – for example, that you are authorized by BaFin as a crypto-asset service provider under MiCA. It becomes a problem when authorization reads like a quality seal: phrases such as "BaFin-approved investment" or authority logos suggest an endorsement that does not exist.
