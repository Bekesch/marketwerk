Content & SEO

Why German content has to be written for Germany

Crypto SEO for Germany means ranking for the terms German-speaking users actually type – and then convincing them with content that reads as if it were written in Germany. Translation alone rarely gets you there. German users search with different terms, often expect more detail and judge your brand by how precise your language is.

Good crypto localization for German audiences starts with decisions. Do you address users with the informal "du" or the formal "Sie"? Do you write "Haltefrist" and "Spekulationsfrist", or translate "holding period" literally? Which trust signals matter most – regulation, fee transparency, security, German-language support? Swiss readers expect "ss" instead of "ß" and prices in CHF, and Austrian German has its own vocabulary.

We build your content engine around those answers: German keyword research, guides and calculators that solve real questions, blog content, owned channels like YouTube and newsletters, and QA for your website and app. The goal is a repeatable process, not a one-time translation project. The same content then feeds your PR and creator campaigns. Our guide to crypto localization for the German market goes into detail.

Isn't a good translation enough for the German market?

Usually not. A translation converts words; localization adapts the message. German users search with different terms, expect precise legal and tax language and look for trust signals such as regulation, fee transparency and German-language support. Literal translations of product and tax terms also miss the keywords people actually use. That's why we start from German search data and write for German readers.

How long does crypto SEO in Germany take to show results?

It depends on your category, your competitors and the state of your site, so we don't promise rankings or timelines. What we can show is how it went for Waltio: in a market where incumbents had a decade-long SEO head start, Waltio reached #3 in organic keyword rankings for crypto tax topics within a few months. Here is how we plan content around tax season.

Should we address German users with du or Sie?

It depends on your brand and audience. Many crypto and fintech brands in Germany use the informal du because it feels direct and modern, while more traditional financial brands keep the formal Sie. What matters most is consistency across website, app, emails and support. We make the decision with you early and document it in the style guide.

Do you localize for Austria and Switzerland too?

Yes, where it makes a difference. Most German content works across DACH, but details differ: Swiss Standard German uses "ss" instead of "ß", prices may need to be shown in CHF, Austrian German has its own vocabulary, and regulation and tax rules differ – Switzerland is not under MiCA. We flag where a separate page or version is worth it, for example for tax or regulatory topics.

Can you work with our in-house team?

Yes. We can produce everything, or provide research, a style guide and editing while your own team writes. We adapt to your setup and leave behind a glossary, briefs and an editorial calendar, so the content engine keeps running with or without us. For tax and regulatory topics, we set up a shared review process with your experts.
