KOL & Influencers

Why crypto creators work differently in Germany

Crypto influencer marketing in Germany works when the right German-speaking creators explain your product to an audience that trusts them – clearly labelled, accurate and repeated often enough to stick. In DACH, that trust comes from education, not hype: users want to understand fees, security and regulation before they sign up.

The creator scene is smaller than in English-speaking markets, and audience quality varies widely. Some channels have real, engaged communities in DACH; others have inflated follower counts or audiences that sit mostly outside the region. Paid posts must be labelled as "Werbung" or "Anzeige" under German unfair competition and media law, and claims about crypto-assets have to stay fair, clear and not misleading under MiCA. Picking creators on reach alone is the fastest way to waste a budget here.

We know this side from the inside: our founder Michael Beches built a German crypto YouTube community with thousands of followers himself. We vet creators on audience data rather than follower counts, favor series and tutorials over one-off posts and connect creator content with PR and your community. Our guide to crypto influencer marketing in Germany goes deeper.

How do you choose German crypto influencers?

We start with fit, not reach: does the creator cover your topic credibly, and does their audience match your target users? Then we check audience location and language, engagement quality, comment sections, past sponsorships and red flags such as bought followers or promotion of questionable projects. Only creators who pass make the shortlist, and you approve the final selection.

Do influencer posts need to be labelled in Germany?

Yes. Paid or otherwise commercially motivated posts must be clearly recognizable as advertising, typically labelled "Werbung" or "Anzeige", under German unfair competition law (UWG) and media law. Similar principles apply in Austria and Switzerland. For crypto-assets, MiCA adds that marketing must be fair, clear and not misleading. We build both into every brief. This is not legal advice.

Which platforms work best for crypto KOL marketing in DACH?

It depends on your product. YouTube suits explainers and tutorials when users need to understand a product before they trust it. X is where the crypto-native audience discusses news and projects, Instagram reaches a broader and less crypto-native audience, and Telegram groups and podcasts build depth with smaller, loyal communities. Most campaigns combine two or three platforms instead of spreading thin.

Why series instead of one-off posts?

One sponsored post rarely changes behavior, especially in a market where users need trust before they sign up. A series – for example a tutorial, a follow-up with results and a Q&A – gives the audience time to understand the product and see the creator actually use it. It also gives us enough data to judge whether a creator works for you.

How do you measure influencer campaigns?

Every creator gets their own tracking links or codes, and we report reach, engagement, clicks, sign-ups and cost per result side by side. Views alone say little; what matters is what happens after the click. Comments and questions from the audience are the second signal, because they show which messages land and which need work.
